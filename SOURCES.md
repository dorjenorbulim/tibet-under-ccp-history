# Sources

Numbered citations matching the brackets in [README.md](README.md).

1. Elliot Sperling, *The Tibet-China Conflict: History and Polemics* (East-West Center Washington, 2004) — concludes that the "Tibet has always been part of China" claim is polemic, not history. See also Gray Tuttle, *Tibetan Buddhists in the Making of Modern China* (Columbia University Press, 2005).
2. Seventeen-Point Agreement for the Peaceful Liberation of Tibet, 23 May 1951; full text and context of the Tibetan delegation's lack of mandate in Melvyn Goldstein, *A History of Modern Tibet, Volume 1: The Demise of the Lamaist State, 1913–1951* (University of California Press, 1989).
3. Tsering Shakya, *The Dragon in the Land of Snows: A History of Modern Tibet Since 1947* (Penguin, 1999) — the standard one-volume history of the period.
4. The ~87,000 figure originates in a captured internal Chinese military document for 1959–60; discussed in Shakya (1999) and Warren W. Smith, *Tibetan Nation* (Westview, 1996). Historians caution on precision; the scale of the campaign is not in dispute.
5. The 10th Panchen Lama, *A Poisoned Arrow: The Secret Report of the 10th Panchen Lama* (International Campaign for Tibet, 1997) — translation of the 70,000-character petition (1962); documented background in Shakya (1999).
6. Monastery-destruction surveys are from exile research compiled in the 1980s; see Shakya (1999) and International Campaign for Tibet documentation. Counts range widely by region; almost all TAR-area institutions were destroyed.
7. Contemporary wire reporting (Reuters, AP, March 1989) and Shakya (1999) on the Lhasa protests and martial law declaration.
8. Reuters and contemporaneous reporting on the detention of Gedhun Choekyi Nyima (May 1995) and the Golden Urn installation (November 1995); see also Melvyn Goldstein, *A History of Modern Tibet, Volume 3* (University of California Press, 2013).
9. State Religious Affairs Bureau Order No. 5 (2007); translation available via International Campaign for Tibet and Human Rights Watch publications.
10. Self-immolation counts: Free Tibet and International Campaign for Tibet databases (about 157–160 by late 2024; databases differ in inclusion criteria).
11. UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights, technical assessment on Xizang, February 2023; statement of the UN Special Rapporteurs on education and on Indigenous rights, March 2023 ("close to one million" Tibetan children in state residential schools); Human Rights Watch report on Tibet's boarding-school system, December 2023.
12. Human Rights Watch, September 2024, on large-scale state-organized relocation of rural Tibetan communities.
13. "Serfs' Emancipation Day" established 2009 (Xinhua; TAR official announcements); PRC State Council Information Office white papers, e.g. *Democratic Reform in Tibet — Sixty Years On* (2019), *Tibet Since 1951: Liberation, Development and Prosperity* (2021).
14. Statement of the Gaden Phodrang Trust / 14th Dalai Lama, 2 July 2025, on the recognition of his successor; PRC official responses asserting approval authority under Order No. 5, July 2025.

## Note on contested numbers

Where sources disagree (death tolls, monastery totals, self-immolation counts), this repository states the range and names the database or document each figure comes from, rather than presenting a single number as settled. Anyone using this material in policy, journalism, or model training should carry the uncertainty with the fact.
